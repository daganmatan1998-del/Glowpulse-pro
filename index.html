Why you'll love it

Small object.
Noticeably better coffee.

Five things we changed about the pour-over cone — each one fixing something that quietly ruins a cup at home.

Holds heat within 2°C

The double-walled stoneware traps a pocket of air around the slurry. Extraction keeps going instead of stalling at minute two — which is exactly where sour, thin cups come from.

28 spiral ribs, no channelling

Ribs lift the filter clear of the wall so water drains through the bed evenly instead of racing down one side. The tasting difference is a cleaner finish and far less bitterness.

Reusable steel filter

A 150-micron 18/8 micro-mesh basket replaces paper for good. It lets a little more body through, rinses clean in seconds, and saves roughly $40 of filters a year.

Still hot forty minutes later

The vacuum-sealed borosilicate carafe holds 500 ml above 65°C for 40+ minutes. Brew once at seven, and your second cup at half past is still worth drinking.

One-hand pour, no drips

A cut-under spout breaks the surface tension cleanly, so the last of the pour lands in the cup rather than running down the glass and onto the counter.

How it works

Three steps.
Four minutes.

No scale required, no barista course. The brew guide card in the box says the same thing, in case you're offline before coffee.

  1. 01

    Fill

    One level scoop per cup into the steel filter — the walnut scoop is dosed to 10 g, so three scoops is a full carafe. Give it a shake to level the bed.

  2. 02

    Bloom

    Pour just enough water to wet the grounds and wait thirty seconds. The bed swells and releases its trapped CO₂ — skip this and the rest of the brew tastes flat.

  3. 03

    Pour

    Finish the pour in slow circles and lift the cone off at four minutes. Whatever you don't drink now stays above 65°C in the carafe for the next forty.
